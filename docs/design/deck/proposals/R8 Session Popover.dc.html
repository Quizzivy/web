A session in the calendar, and what a teacher can do with it

The deck draws the Calendar and its New session form. Pressing a class session there jumps to Attendance, and nothing else can be done to a session: not moved, not cancelled, not stopped. R8 builds those (T-R8.4, T-R8.11) and its plan asks for a popover “from deck primitives” (DG-92). This page draws it: the popover on the week grid and as a sheet on a phone, its six actions, the two dialogs and the three confirms, and how a cancelled session looks on the grid. Everything is in Vietnamese first. One thing goes beyond the plan’s wording and is a question below: a sentence in the repeating-session dialog that says what a change does to dates already cancelled or moved.

The popover What the session is, then what can be done to it.
  • Five lines of fact: the class, the date, the time, the room, and whether it repeats. A note and a mark for “cancelled” or “changed” follow when there is one.
  • The actions are a menu, one per line, in the plan’s order. Only those that apply are listed: a one-off has no “repeating” actions, and “Điểm danh” appears once the session has started.
  • On a phone it is a sheet from the bottom, with the same lines and 44px rows.
One date, or every week The two are never mixed in one form.
  • “Sửa buổi này” and “Huỷ buổi này” touch one date. “Sửa lịch lặp lại” and “Dừng lặp lại” touch the weeks to come. Each opens its own dialog, and each dialog says which it is in its first line.
  • A change to the weeks to come starts at a date, the next session by default, and the dialog says that everything before it stays.
  • It also says, before saving, that dates already moved or cancelled from then on will follow the new schedule. The plan tells the teacher only afterwards, as a count.
Cancelling A cancelled session stays on the grid, struck through.
  • It is not removed: students see it as cancelled in “Tuần này”, and the teacher can restore it from the same popover.
  • The confirm’s two buttons read “Giữ buổi này” and “Huỷ buổi”. The usual “Huỷ” would mean both “do not do this” and “cancel the session”.
  • A one-off that nobody has taken attendance for can be deleted instead. Anything with attendance can only be cancelled, and the confirm says so if it comes to that.
